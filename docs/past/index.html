How this page is meant to be used

The old site had a thin page for every year from 1960 to 2024, each with the same title and about 30 words. Those URLs collected impressions — “if I was born in 1998, how old am I?” — and almost no clicks. This preview puts that job on one hub. If you arrive with a year in the query string, the past date is pre-filled as 1 January of that year, or today if the year is the current one, and the note above the form says so.

The count is the same as the homepage: completed years, leftover months, leftover days, with 1 March as the civil anniversary of a 29 February birthday in a common year. A person born on 15 March 1995 was, on 1 January 2010, 14 years, 9 months and 17 days old.

If you want the gap between two people’s birthdays, use age difference. If you only want the number of days between two calendar dates, use days between dates.

Questions people actually ask

If I only know the year, which date should I use?

This page treats “in 1990” as 1 January 1990 unless you change it. That default is shown when you open a year link, because a year is not a single day.

Can I use a date in the future?

You can, and the arithmetic is the same, but the How old will I be? page is written for that question.
