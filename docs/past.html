When you need this

Forms ask it more than people do: “age at the date of the incident”, “age when employment began”, “age at diagnosis”. They nearly always mean completed years on that day, which is what the big number shows. If the form wants months as well, the line underneath has them.

Someone born on 15 March 1995 was 14 years, 9 months and 17 days old on 1 January 2010. They had their 14th birthday the March before, and they turned 15 two and a half months later.

Coming from an old year link?

Older pages on this site covered whole years, like “how old was I in 1990”. A year isn’t a single day, so links like that open this page set to 1 January of the year, and say so above the result. Change it to the day you actually mean.

Leap days and month ends

A 29 February birthday counts on 1 March in years without one, and a month after 31 January is 1 March. That’s the same rule as the main age calculator, so the two pages always agree.

Questions people ask

How old was I in 1990?

It depends on whether your birthday had come yet. On 1 January 1990 you were 1990 minus your birth year, minus one; from your birthday that year you were one older. Type your date of birth above to see both.

How does this calculator count a month, or a 29 February birthday?

A completed month lands on the same date next month. If that date doesn’t exist — there is no 31 February, and some years have no 29 February — it rolls to the 1st of the next month, so 31 January plus one month is 1 March, and a 29 February birthday counts on 1 March in a common year. Common date libraries (for example date-fns, Python’s dateutil and Java’s LocalDate) use the last day of the month instead, so around month ends other tools can differ by a day or two.
