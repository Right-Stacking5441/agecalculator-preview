Why this is not times seven

The “one dog year equals seven human years” line is a rough average that does not fit puppies or old dogs, and it ignores size. A common veterinary rule of thumb, and the one used here, is: the first year is about 15 human years, the second adds about 9 (so 24 at the end of year two), and each year after that adds 4 for a small dog, 5 for medium, 6 for large, or 7 for giant. A five-year-old medium dog is about 39 human years on this table, not 35.

Those later-year increments are a size-group shorthand, not a breed-by-breed study. A 2019 paper on Labrador methylation used a logarithmic curve, which is a different model and is not what this form applies. Treat the number as a rough comparison, not a veterinary diagnosis. If you want a person’s civil age, use the age calculator. Cats have a different curve on the cat age page.
