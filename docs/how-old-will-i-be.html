Type a date and the result appears here as you go.

A completed month lands on the same date next month. If that date doesn’t exist — 31 January to 1 March, or a 29 February birthday in a common year — it rolls to the 1st of the next month. Some software, including common date libraries, uses the last day of the month instead, so results can differ by a day around month ends.

Type your date of birth and a year, and the page answers “how old will I be” in that year: the age you reach, the date you reach it, and the age you are before that date. The year box starts at 2050, so the first answer you see is for 2050. Change it to any year from 1000 to 2999.

The result also lists your age on 1 January and on 31 December of that year, the weekday your birthday falls on, and how many days there are from today until it. If you’d rather know your age on one particular day, press Change and pick an exact date instead. The result then switches to years, months and days, the same as the main age calculator.

Two ages in one year

Everyone is two ages during a year, one before their birthday and one after, unless they were born on 1 January. That’s why a single number for “how old will I be in 2050” can be wrong for half the year.

A worked example

Someone born on 15 March 1995 asks how old they will be in 2050.

  • 2050 minus 1995 is 55. That is the age they reach on their birthday that year.
  • Their birthday in 2050 is Tuesday 15 March.
  • Before that, from 1 January to 14 March 2050, they are still 54.

So they are 54 at the start of 2050 and 55 from 15 March onwards, and they finish the year at 55. As of 16 November 2026, that birthday is 8,520 days away.

For an exact date, the same person on 1 January 2040 will be 44 years, 9 months and 17 days old: 44 from their birthday in March 2039, then nine months to 15 December, then 17 days.

Born on 29 February

This calculator counts a 29 February birthday as falling on 1 March in common years. That is the rule in Scottish law under the Age of Legal Capacity (Scotland) Act 1991, section 6(2); the law in England, Wales and Northern Ireland does not name a date. 2050 isn’t a leap year, so someone born on 29 February 2000 turns 50 on Tuesday 1 March 2050, and the page says the birthday falls on 1 March. In a leap year such as 2048, it falls on the 29th. All the tools on this site use the same rule, including the age calculator.

Why people ask “how old will I be?”

Mostly for planning. How old will I be when the mortgage is paid off, when the children leave school, when a pension can be drawn, or when a passport or driving licence next needs renewing. If the plan hangs on a specific day, use the exact date option, because “the year I turn 60” covers two different ages.

People also ask it for fun about milestone years like 2030, 2050 or 2100. The year box takes any year from 1000 to 2999.

If the year you type is before you were born, the page says so instead of giving a negative age. A year that has already gone works too; the “days from today” line just says the birthday has already passed. For dates in the past, though, how old was I is set up for the job. To work out a birth year from an age rather than the other way round, use what year was I born.

Questions people ask

How old will I be in 2050?

Subtract your birth year from 2050. That’s the age you reach on your birthday in 2050; before that day you’ll be one younger. Born in 1995, you turn 55 in 2050 and are 54 until your birthday.

How do I work out my age on a future date?

Subtract your birth year from the year of the date, then take one off if your birthday in that year falls after the date. For months and days as well, use the exact date option.

What if I was born on 29 February?

This calculator counts your birthday as 1 March in years without a 29 February, and the 29th itself in leap years.

Can I check my age on a specific future day?

Yes. Press Change next to “Or pick” and type the date. The result then gives years, months and days on that day.
