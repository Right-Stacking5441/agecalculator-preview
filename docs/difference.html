How the gap is worked out

The gap is counted from the earlier birthday to the later one, the same way an age is: whole years, then whole months, then the days left over. Someone born on 2 June 1990 is 3 years, 3 months and 18 days older than someone born on 20 September 1993, which is 1,206 days.

That number of days never changes. The difference in their ages, as you’d say it out loud, does. From 2 June to 19 September each year the older one is 4 years older on paper (say 36 and 32), and for the rest of the year it’s 3 (36 and 33). Both are true, which is why a precise gap is more useful than subtracting one age from another.

Two people with the same birthday

If both dates are the same day and month, the gap is a whole number of years and stays that way all year. Twins born either side of midnight can be a day apart and have different birthdays, which this page will show as 1 day.

Questions people ask

Is the age difference the same every year?

In days, yes. In whole years it changes by one for part of each year, between the two birthdays.

Can I compare more than two people?

Not yet on one screen. Work out the gap for each pair, or compare everyone with the eldest.
