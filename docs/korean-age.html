The date is today in your time zone, and you can change it to see any of the three on another day, such as a past New Year’s Day.

What the Korean age calculator counts

International age (만 나이, man nai) starts at 0 when you’re born and goes up by one on each birthday. It’s the age used in most of the world and everywhere else on this site, including the main age calculator.

Korean age (세는 나이, se-neun nai) starts at 1 on the day you’re born, and everyone gets a year older together on 1 January, not on their birthday. A baby born on 31 December is 1 that day and 2 the next morning.

Year age (연 나이, yeon nai), sometimes called calendar age, is the current year minus your birth year. Birthdays don’t come into it, so someone born in 1995 is 31 for the whole of 2026.

The simple rule of thumb: Korean age is international age plus one if your birthday has already passed this year, and plus two if it hasn’t. Year age sits in between.

A worked example

As of 16 November 2026, someone born on 20 December 1995 has three different ages:

  • International age: their 2026 birthday is still a month away, so they’re 30.
  • Korean age: 2026 minus 1995 is 31, plus 1 for being counted as 1 at birth, so 32.
  • Year age: 2026 minus 1995, so 31.

On 20 December 2026 their international age becomes 31, and the other two stay where they are. On 1 January 2027, Korean age goes to 33 and year age to 32, while international age stays at 31 until the next December.

What changed in June 2023

Laws amending South Korea’s Civil Act and the Framework Act on Public Administration (행정기본법) took effect on 28 June 2023. From that date, ages in laws, contracts and official documents are read as international age unless a specific law says otherwise. The Ministry of Government Legislation’s announcement, published on korea.kr (in Korean), sets out the change, and Reuters reported that South Koreans became a year or two younger on paper that day.

International age was not new for official use. According to the Law Library of Congress, it was already the legal age in principle, for things like voting and driving, though some individual laws, such as the Youth Protection Act and the Military Service Act, counted in year age. What the 2023 laws did was make international age the default, so a rule or a form that doesn’t say which age it means is read as international age. Laws that say otherwise keep their own rule.

Some rules still use year age. A briefing by the Ministry of Government Legislation, published on the government’s korea.kr site (in Korean), names school starting age, the age for buying alcohol and tobacco under the Youth Protection Act, eligibility for civil service exams and military service duty as exceptions kept for now in June 2023, because they work in whole years. Korean age also lives on in conversation, especially among older people and when working out who is senior to whom.

This page explains how the counting works. It is not legal advice. For anything with legal weight in Korea, check the wording of the rule itself.

Why three systems caused trouble

With three ages in use, a number on its own was ambiguous. The Law Library of Congress gives the example of a COVID-19 vaccine label in South Korea that said “not recommended for those under the age of 30”, which led many people to ask whether that meant Korean age or international age. If someone tells you their Korean age today, take two off to get their international age if their birthday hasn’t come yet this year, or one if it has.

Questions people ask

How do I calculate my Korean age?

Take the current year, subtract your birth year, and add 1. Your birthday makes no difference. Someone born in 1995 is 32 in Korean age throughout 2026.

Why am I two years older in Korean age?

Before your birthday in a given year, Korean age is two more than international age: one for being counted as 1 at birth, and one for the new year that has already passed.

Does South Korea still use Korean age?

Not for legal or official purposes. Since 28 June 2023 international age is the default in Korean law, so ages in laws, contracts and official documents are read that way unless a rule says otherwise. A few rules, such as buying alcohol and tobacco (Youth Protection Act) and military service, still went by year age as of the 2023 change, and Korean age is still used informally.

What is year age?

The current year minus your birth year, with no birthday involved. It’s the middle number in the result.
