Three ways of counting

International age starts at 0 and goes up on your birthday. It’s what this site shows everywhere else.

Korean age (세는 나이) starts at 1 on the day you’re born, and everyone gets a year older on 1 January. A baby born on 31 December is 2 the next day.

Year age (연 나이) is the current year minus your birth year, with no birthday involved. Someone born in 1995 is 31 throughout 2026.

What changed in 2023

From 28 June 2023, South Korea standardised on international age for legal and administrative purposes. Some rules, such as those for military service and for buying alcohol and tobacco, still go by year age, and Korean age is still common in conversation. This page explains the counting. For anything legal, check the rule itself.

Questions people ask

Why am I two years older in Korean age?

If your birthday hasn’t come yet this year, Korean age is two more than international age: one for being born at 1, and one for the new year.
