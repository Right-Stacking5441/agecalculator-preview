## Body

## What you get

Cats only need an age, in years and months. The result is an estimate in human years, plus the life stage vets use for a cat of that age. Before you type an age, the result card has a short chart of common ages under "Cat age chart".

These are estimates, not measurements. Cats age at different rates depending on genetics, health and how they live, and no conversion can tell you how a particular cat is doing. For questions about your cat's health, ask your vet.

## How cat years are counted

The calculator looks your cat's age up on the [pet age chart published on the American Animal Hospital Association website](https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/canine-life-stage-2019/canine_and_feline_age_chart_poster.pdf), a Zoetis poster whose cat column is adapted from the 2010 AAFP-AAHA Feline Life Stage Guidelines. On that chart a cat is 10 at six months, 15 at one year and 24 at two, and every year after that adds 4.

Cats under 6 months aren't shown, because the chart starts there. It stops at 21 years, so the calculator does too rather than make up figures past the end.

| Cat's age | Human years |
|---:|---:|
| 6 months | 10 |
| 1 | 15 |
| 2 | 24 |
| 3 | 28 |
| 5 | 36 |
| 7 | 44 |
| 10 | 56 |
| 15 | 76 |
| 20 | 96 |
| 21 | 100 |

Months count too. Between two printed ages the calculator reads in a straight line, so a cat of 4 years and 6 months is halfway between the chart's 32 at four and 36 at five, which is 34. That straight-line reading is this site's method, not the chart's.

## Why cats don't need a size setting

Dogs range from a 3 kg Chihuahua to a Great Dane ten times heavier or more, and big dogs age noticeably faster, so the [dog age calculator](/dog-age) asks for size. Cats vary much less in size, and one curve fits most of them. The American Veterinary Medical Association makes the same distinction in its [senior pets guidance](https://www.avma.org/resources/pet-owners/petcare/senior-pets): cats are generally considered senior after 10, while dogs have no single senior age because of their wider range of sizes.

## Life stages

The [2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-aafp-feline-life-stage-guidelines/), from the American Animal Hospital Association and the American Association of Feline Practitioners, divide a cat's life into four stages:

- Kitten: birth to 1 year
- Young adult: 1 to 6 years
- Mature adult: 7 to 10 years
- Senior: over 10 years

The [guidelines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10812130/) add a fifth stage, end of life, which can come at any age.

The result shows which stage your cat is in. The guidelines link each stage to different care. Mature cats start being screened for age-related problems such as kidney disease, and seniors are watched more closely, with more frequent check-ups. The AVMA suggests senior pets see a vet twice a year or more.

## A cat of exactly 10

Every cat is counted the same way: 15 human years for the first year, 9 for the second and 4 for each year after that.

A cat that is exactly 10 years old:

- First year: 15.
- Second year: 9 more, so 24.
- Years three to ten: 8 years at 4 each, so 32 more.

24 + 32 = 56 human years. On the AAHA/AAFP stages a 10-year-old is still a mature adult, and counts as a senior once they are past their 10th birthday. For comparison, the old "times seven" rule would say 70, which overstates it.

## Indoor, outdoor and very old cats

Where your cat lives and what they eat may change how long they live, but the chart doesn't adjust for it, and neither does this calculator. It converts age and nothing more. On the chart an 18-year-old cat is 88 in human years, and a 21-year-old, the last age printed, is 100.

## Compared with other conversions
### Cat years and dog years
For small dogs the chart figures are the same as for cats at every age. Medium, large and giant dogs age faster after their second year.

### The times-seven rule for cats
It undercounts young cats and overcounts older ones. A one-year-old cat is about 15 in human years rather than 7, and a 10-year-old about 56 rather than 70.
