## Body

## One birthday, three numbers

Put one date of birth into the Korean age calculator and you get three numbers. The big one is international age, the default in Korean law since 28 June 2023. Beside it are traditional Korean age (세는 나이), still heard in everyday life, and year age (연 나이), which a few rules, such as buying alcohol and tobacco and military service, used when the change came in. The date is today in your time zone, and you can change it to see any of the three on another day, such as a past New Year's Day.

## Three ways of counting

**International age (만 나이, man nai)** starts at 0 when you're born and goes up by one on each birthday. It's the age used in most of the world and everywhere else on this site, including the main [age calculator](/).

**Korean age (세는 나이, se-neun nai)** starts at 1 on the day you're born, and everyone gets a year older together on 1 January, not on their birthday. A baby born on 31 December is 1 that day and 2 the next morning.

**Year age (연 나이, yeon nai)**, sometimes called calendar age, is the current year minus your birth year. Birthdays don't come into it, so someone born in 1995 is 31 for the whole of 2026.

The simple rule of thumb: Korean age is international age plus one if your birthday has already passed this year, and plus two if it hasn't. Year age sits in between.

## Born on 20 December 1995

Take someone born on 20 December 1995. On 16 November 2026 they would have three different ages:

- International age: their 2026 birthday would still be a month away, so they would be 30.
- Korean age: 2026 minus 1995 is 31, plus 1 for being counted as 1 at birth, so 32.
- Year age: 2026 minus 1995, so 31.

Their international age would go up to 31 on 20 December 2026, and the other two would stay where they are. On 1 January 2027 Korean age would go up to 33 and year age to 32, while international age would stay at 31 until 20 December 2027.

## What changed in June 2023

Laws amending South Korea's Civil Act and the Framework Act on Public Administration (행정기본법) took effect on 28 June 2023. From that date, ages in laws, contracts and official documents are read as international age unless a specific law says otherwise. The [Ministry of Government Legislation's announcement](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148916838), published on korea.kr (in Korean), sets out the change, and [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/south-koreans-become-year-or-two-younger-traditional-way-counting-age-scrapped-2023-06-28/) that South Koreans became a year or two younger on paper that day.

International age was not new for official use. According to the [Law Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2023/01/koreans-becoming-younger-unification-of-age-counting-systems/), it was already the legal age in principle, for things like voting and driving, though some individual laws, such as the Youth Protection Act and the Military Service Act, counted in year age. What the 2023 laws did was make international age the default, so a rule or a form that doesn't say which age it means is read as international age. Laws that say otherwise keep their own rule.

Some rules still use year age. In a [briefing on 26 June 2023](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=156577051), published on the government's korea.kr site (in Korean), the Minister of Government Legislation said a few exceptions would be kept for now: school starting age, the age for buying alcohol and tobacco under the Youth Protection Act, the age for sitting civil service exams, and military service duty. School entry and military service, he said, are run a whole year at a time. Korean age also lives on in conversation, especially among older people and when working out who is senior to whom.

This page explains how the counting works. It is not legal advice. For anything with legal weight in Korea, check the wording of the rule itself.

## Why three systems caused trouble

With three ages in use, a number on its own was ambiguous. The Law Library of Congress gives the example of a COVID-19 vaccine label in South Korea that said "not recommended for those under the age of 30", which led many people to ask whether that meant Korean age or international age. If someone tells you their Korean age today, take two off to get their international age if their birthday hasn't come yet this year, or one if it has.
